## 9. Results and Evaluation

### 9.1 Evaluation Metrics

To quantitatively assess localization accuracy, the estimated trajectory from LIO-SAM's keyframes ($p_i$) was compared against CARLA's ground-truth odometry ($q_i$). Evaluating SLAM trajectories requires aligning the coordinate frames since LIO-SAM computes poses relative to its local initialization origin, while CARLA uses absolute world coordinates.

We apply the **Umeyama closed-form alignment method (SE(2))** [7] to find the optimal rotation matrix $R$ and translation vector $t$ that minimizes the mean squared error between the aligned trajectories:

$$\min_{R, t} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left\| q_i - (R p_i + t) \right\|^2$$

The principal evaluation metric is the **Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE**:

$$\text{ATE}_{\text{RMSE}} = \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left\| q_i - \hat{p}_i \right\|^2 }$$

where $\hat{p}_i = R p_i + t$ represents the aligned LIO-SAM pose. The mean absolute error (MAE), median error, and maximum error are also computed.

### 9.2 Controlled Experiments

To evaluate the robustness of the SLAM pipeline, two tests were conducted on the same Town10HD route:

1. **Static Environment (Clean Bag):** Run with no dynamic traffic (NPC vehicles or pedestrians). Route length: **597.4 m** over **133.4 seconds**.
2. **Dynamic Environment (Traffic Bag):** Run with 10 NPC vehicles driving autonomously in traffic lanes. Route length: **374.4 m** over **123.3 seconds**.

The quantitative trajectory alignment metrics for both scenarios are presented below:

| LIO-SAM Keyframes | 378 | 190 |

### 9.5 Analysis of Collision-Induced Trajectory Failure vs. Dynamic Traffic Robustness

The quantitative results demonstrate that in the second experiment (Static/Clean Bag), the pipeline achieved an exceptionally low trajectory drift of **0.42%** over a 597.4 m path. This serves as a baseline representing the sensor-fusion performance under nominal conditions.

In the first experiment (Traffic Bag), the relative error rose to **4.05%** (ATE RMSE of 15.17 m). A detailed analysis of the playback log reveals that this performance drop-off was **not** caused by standard scan-matching drift from moving vehicles. In fact, LIO-SAM proved to be highly robust to normal dynamic traffic in Town10HD. The degradation was instead caused by a discrete simulation anomaly: **a non-player character (NPC) vehicle spawned directly on top of the ego vehicle** during the run.

This physical collision degraded the tracking accuracy through three distinct factors:
1. **IMU Saturation:** The sudden high-g impact caused a measurement spike that saturated the simulated accelerometer and gyroscope. The integrated state estimation used for IMU pre-integration was temporarily corrupted, leading to an incorrect motion forecast for the next scan match.
2. **Close-Range LiDAR Occlusion:** During the collision, the body of the spawning vehicle completely blocked the LiDAR's field of view at zero range. The point cloud was filled with returns from the occluding vehicle instead of static street geometry, causing the scan-to-map (ICP) optimization to slip.
3. **Permanent Translation Offset:** Once the vehicle recovered and separated from the obstacle, LIO-SAM successfully re-localized and stabilized its scan matching. However, the coordinate slip introduced during the collision remained as a permanent absolute translation offset, raising the overall ATE RMSE metric.

This evaluation demonstrates that while the pipeline is robust to moving traffic, localized high-shock events and sensor occlusion from collisions present a vulnerability. Integrating absolute references (like GPS/GNSS) or collision-recovery re-localization is necessary to handle such edge cases in real-world scenarios.
